Before: Fisetin has gained a lot of attention for its function as a senolytic compound. Senolytics are drugs or compounds that selectively target and eliminate senescent ‘zombie’ cells, which are cells that have stopped dividing and are no longer functioning properly. These cells can accumulate in the body with age and contribute to inflammation and age-related diseases. Studies have shown that fisetin may help selectively eliminate senescent cells in mice and improve markers of health and longevity. For example, a study published in the journal eBioMedicine found that fisetin supplementation in mice improved physical function, reduced markers of inflammation, and increased lifespan 1 . Of the 10 flavonoids tested, fisetin was the most potent senolytic. In addition, there are several other pathways in which Fisetin may help support healthy aging: Chronic inflammation has been linked to a variety of age-related diseases, and fisetin has been shown to help reduce inflammation in the body 2–4 . Fisetin is a powerful antioxidant, which means it helps to neutralise harmful free radicals in the body that can damage cells and contribute to aging. Fisetin functions, at least in part, via interactions with redox signalling pathways, and modulation of the NF-κB, Nrf2, MAPK and PI3K/Akt signalling pathways 5,6 . Fisetin has been shown to help support healthy cellular function and to protect DNA from damage 7,8 , which can contribute to cellular aging and disease. While the exact mechanisms by which fisetin may increase longevity are still being studied, it is clear that this natural flavonoid has a variety of health benefits that support healthy aging.
